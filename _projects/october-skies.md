October Skies is a series of much smaller test rockets meant to test a specific aspect of the main competition rocket.

In 2018, we launched our first October Skies, named after the [1999 film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Sky).
As we built more and more rockets, October Skies became almost an annual tradition to the team.

In 2019, another October Skies was built to test the new custom parachutes and exploding eyebolt, which were later incorporated in [Jackalope](/projects/jackalope).
Like the 2018 October Skies, the 2019 one was also donated to one of our many [sponsors](/sponsors).

Despite the challenges during the pandemic, we remained dedicated to build October Skies in 2020. The 2020 October Skies tested the payload recovery quadcopter.
This quadcopter has the ability to fold out and return the sample to the launch site, as the sample would only have been viable for 2 hours.
Unfortunately, the drone was lost in a snowstorm during testing.

October Skies finally stepped into the competitive stage at LC25! Competing in the Technology Development Category, this October Skies is a small G-class motor rocket designed to house our ARGUS sensor suite.

After [Apogee-Gee](/projects/apogeegee) suffered from melted stickers that led to an increase in skin friction drag, we wanted to develop a better understanding at what rockets experience in flight, particularly pressure, temperature and strain data, and subsequently comparing these results to CFD analysis.

At Launch Canada 2025, the team presented October Skies and ARGUS at the conference. However, with the rapid unscheduled disassembly of [Apogee-Gee](/projects/apogeegee), most of our personnel went into an extensive recovery operation and a launch in Timmins was not viable.
For our demonstration, we were awarded 3rd place in the Technology Development Category, our team’s first podium finish at Launch Canada!

In November 2025, October Skies was finally completed and fully calibrated for launch. While it experienced a nominal flight, the extreme weather unfortunately led to no data points collected.
In order to focus our resources on the upcoming liquid engine rocket project, our avionics sub-team, consisting of many new recruits, has decided to start anew by developing a brand-new avionics architecture, the [NGAP](/current-projects#NGAP).
