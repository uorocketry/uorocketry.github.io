At our inception in 2016, uORocketry, or uOSTAR (University of Ottawa Student Team of Aeronautics and Rocketry) at the time, was focused on developing a hybrid engine.

The first hybrid engine underwent static fire in just 6 months after the team’s foundation, with our founding members personally funding this project.

In 2019, a second generation of our hybrid engine was developed. However, we were hit hard by COVID, and this engine was never completed.

Thankfully, in 2020, we began developing our third hybrid engine—*Inkpod*, aimed to burn nitrous oxide and paraffin wax with additive to improve regression. For the next two years, we carried out two cold flows with Inkpod and continued to refine our designs.

The team also moved away from Spaceport America Cup 2022 and focused on supporting the Canada-based competition, the Launch Canada Challenge. At LC22, due to technical difficulties, we pivoted our hot fire demonstration to a cold flow demonstration, which yielded nominal results!

*Inkpod* also served as the basis for the scrapped Rocktopus rocket. Rocktopus was designed during COVID and was meant to house the Inkpod hybrid engine and an E. coli experiment developed with the Faculty of Biology. With a shift in design focus to [Gee-Force One](/projects/geeforce-one), the essence of Rocktopus lives on as our CAN-RGX entry.

Since then, we have altered our propulsion target to a liquid engine, which serves as our current 2-year plans in incorporating Incendium Terrae into a full liquid engine rocket. See [Current Projects](/current-projects)!
